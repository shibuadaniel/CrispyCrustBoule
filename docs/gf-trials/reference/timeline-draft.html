✦ Not baked yet. Photos go here after the bake.

What changed in v1.4

  • Water 260 g → 275 g. That takes hydration from about 88% to about 92%. A slightly looser dough expands more in the oven. The psyllium still gels for 10 minutes.
  • Warm rise must reach 25–30% before the fridge. A strip of tape on the pan or banneton at the dough's starting level shows the rise. It gets as long as it needs.
  • Optional: 5 more minutes uncovered at 425°F if the sides still look pale once the inside is at temperature.

Why: v1.3 was the best loaf yet, but I'd like a little more oven spring. Two things limit spring here: a fairly stiff dough, and the warm rise stopping at about 15% instead of 25%. The extra water is the one real recipe change. The proof target was already in v1.3 and is now spelled out so it actually gets hit. Everything else is exactly as v1.3.

Everything else carries over from v1.3.

How it baked

Not baked yet. Things to watch: how the dough handles at 275 g water, how long the warm rise takes to reach 25–30%, the oven spring compared with v1.3, the crumb, and the side colour.

  1. Mix the flour.

    Combine equal parts light buckwheat, sorghum and brown rice flour. Mixing a bigger batch ahead and weighing 100 g from it is easiest. Weigh the 20 g KA GF flour separately.

  2. Feed.

    Stir 50 g starter, 20 g KA GF flour, 100 g flour mix and 120 g water together until smooth. Mark the level on the jar.

  3. Wait for peak.

    Leave it covered at room temperature until it's bubbly, domed and well risen. Use it at or just before peak.

Baker's % is against the 360 g flour blend. Hydration of ~92% (was ~88%) counts the water, the starter's water (100% starter, so 40 g water in 80 g) and the egg whites' water.

Method

  1. Gel the psyllium.

    Whisk the psyllium into the 275 g water right away so it doesn't clump, then let it sit 10 minutes.

  2. Wet mix.

    Whisk in the egg whites and starter.

  3. Add dry.

    Whisk the three flours and salt together, then mix them in until no dry spots remain. The dough should be thick and cohesive. It will firm up as it rests, so don't add water yet.

  4. Rest.

    Cover and leave 30 minutes so the flours and psyllium hydrate. It will be a little softer and stickier than v1.3, which is expected. Only if it still feels stiff or cracks when pressed, work in water a tablespoon at a time.

  5. Shape.

    With wet hands, shape it and set it in a parchment-lined banneton or loaf pan. Smooth the top with a wet spatula. Stick a strip of tape on the outside of the pan or banneton level with the top of the dough.

  6. Warm rise to 25–30%.Changed

    Cover and set it in the oven with only the light on (about 75–80°F). Let it rise 25–30% above the tape: a gentle dome forms, and a fingertip dent springs back slowly. Go by the rise, not the clock. It may take noticeably longer than last time. Don't take it past about 30%, because it keeps rising a little in the fridge.

    Last time it went into the fridge at about 15%.

  7. Cold proof overnight.

    Move it, still covered, to the fridge at 38–40°F overnight.

  8. Preheat.

    In the morning, take the dough out of the fridge first, then heat the oven to 450°F (230°C) with the Dutch oven inside for 45–60 minutes. If the dough still looks flat once the oven is hot, leave it on the counter another 1–2 hours before baking.

  9. Score.

    Just before baking, dust the top with rice flour and make one confident slash about 1 cm deep, holding the blade at a shallow angle.

  10. Bake covered.

    Lower the loaf into the Dutch oven on its parchment, put the lid on, and bake 30 minutes at 450°F.

  11. Bake uncovered, cooler.Changed

    Take the lid off and drop the oven to 425°F (220°C). Bake 10–15 minutes more, just until the inside reads 205–210°F (96–99°C), then pull it right away.

    Optional (new in v1.4): if the sides still look pale once it's at temperature, give it up to 5 minutes more.

  12. Soften the crust (optional).

    While it's hot, brush the crust with a little oil or melted butter, then cool it under a clean towel. Once it's fully cool, keep it in a bag.

  13. Cool.

    Let it cool on a rack for at least 2 hours before cutting. Cutting early makes it gummy.

v1.3

Best yet

Shorter, cooler lid-off bake and an overnight cold proof.

Yield
1 loaf
Hands-on
~30 min
Proof
~25% warm + overnight cold
Bake
40–45 min
Hydration

What changed in v1.3

  • Shorter uncovered bake: 20–25 min → 10–15 min. Still 30 minutes with the lid on, then lid off only until the loaf reads 205–210°F inside.
  • Oven drops to 425°F when the lid comes off (was 450°F the whole bake).
  • Overnight cold proof. A warm rise to about 25% in the oven with only the light on (~75–80°F), then the fridge overnight at 38–40°F, baked straight from cold. This replaces the 2–4 h room-temperature proof to ~50%.
  • Scoring spelled out, plus optional crust-softening steps after the bake.

Why: The last two loaves had a very hard crust. Less time uncovered at a lower heat means a thinner, softer crust. The cold proof fits the bake into the next morning. The dough and starter feed are exactly as in v1.2.

Everything else carries over from v1.2.

How it baked

Best loaf yet. Oval loaf, the score opened fully, good oven spring, even golden top, pale sides. The warm rise only reached about 15% before the fridge. Wanted a bit more spring.

✦ This recipe wasn't baked, so there are no photos.

What changed in v1

  • Ground flax 18 g → psyllium husk powder 15 g. Gel it in the water for 10 minutes, the same way as the flax. Psyllium gives GF dough a stretchy, shapeable body, so it should hold gas and keep its rise.
  • Water 290 g → 260 g to start. Psyllium firms up as it sits, so add water back only if the dough is stiff after the 30-minute rest.

Why: v0 didn't rise enough and was too sticky to shape. Both point to too little structure. Everything else is unchanged, to see what these two changes do.

Everything else carries over from v0.

v1.1 · 21 Nov 2026

The starter feed was added to the card: 50 g starter + 100 g flour mix + 110 g water (110% hydration), with the flour mix in equal thirds of light buckwheat, sorghum and brown rice flour. With a 110% starter, overall hydration is ~89% instead of ~88%. The dough itself didn't change.
